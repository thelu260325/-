## 2. Background and Technological Evolution

讲述人：队友A

【PPT第4页｜队友A接讲｜技术演进：从1G语音到6G融合系统】

**Thank you.**

To understand 6G, it helps to look at what earlier generations changed. First-generation networks supported analog voice calls. Second-generation networks introduced digital voice and messaging. Third-generation networks expanded mobile internet access, while fourth-generation networks made mobile broadband central to everyday life.

Fifth-generation networks broadened the focus beyond smartphones. Their main service categories include enhanced mobile broadband, highly reliable communication with low delay, and connections for large numbers of devices. [2]

Why investigate another generation? Future applications may require several capabilities together: detailed immersive content, rapid responses, accurate positioning, and dependable connectivity while users move.

【切到PPT第5页｜IMT-2030框架；最后强调框架是发展目标】

The ITU's IMT-2030 framework reflects this broader ambition. Alongside improvements to communication, it includes artificial intelligence and communication, integrated sensing and communication, and ubiquitous connectivity. Sustainability and security are also central concerns. [1]

However, a framework is not a finished commercial system. We should treat proposed 6G capabilities as development goals, rather than assume every future connection will achieve every target simultaneously.

## 3. Core Technologies of 6G

讲述人：队友A

【PPT第6页｜队友A继续｜四类核心技术总览】

**With this framework in mind, let’s turn to the core technologies of 6G.**

I will focus on four groups of technologies.

【第6页停留片刻指示四个技术模块，再切到PPT第7页｜AI-native networking与New radio approaches】

The first is AI-native networking. The idea is to design network functions with artificial intelligence in mind from the beginning. Learning algorithms could predict traffic, allocate resources, or help maintain connections as conditions change. Letaief and colleagues describe a two-way relationship: AI can improve wireless networks, and wireless networks can support distributed AI. Edge computing complements this approach by processing some data closer to users. However, AI also requires training data, computing resources, and reliable decisions. [3]

The second group concerns radio capacity and the propagation environment. Sub-terahertz and terahertz research explores wider bandwidths for demanding applications. Large antenna arrays can direct signals towards users, while reconfigurable intelligent surfaces can adjust how radio waves are reflected. These approaches are promising, but they face blockage, propagation loss, and hardware constraints. Higher frequencies would therefore complement lower-frequency coverage, rather than solve every connectivity problem. [2]

【切到PPT第8页｜ISAC与Integrated coverage：感知通信一体化、地面与非地面网络融合】

The third is integrated sensing and communication, or ISAC. A wireless system would transmit information while also using radio signals to estimate properties such as an object's location or movement. Liu and colleagues explain that sharing signals and infrastructure can bring benefits, but sensing accuracy and communication performance may compete for resources. [4]

Finally, researchers are investigating closer integration between terrestrial networks, satellites, and aerial platforms. This could extend connectivity beyond conventional ground infrastructure. The difficulty is coordinating very different links, especially when satellites, aircraft, and users are moving. [2]

## 4. Applications and Case Studies

讲述人：你

【PPT第9页｜你再次接讲｜注意：顶部写07 / APPLICATIONS，右下角实际页码为09】

**Thank you.**

What could these technologies mean in practice? I will use three literature-based application cases. They illustrate proposed designs, not completed commercial 6G deployments.

【仍在PPT第9页｜左侧CASE 01：沉浸式教育；需求 → 技术帮助 → 评价指标】

The first is immersive education. Consider students sharing a virtual laboratory. High-quality images require substantial capacity, while interaction requires consistent response times. Edge computing could support nearby rendering and processing, and intelligent resource allocation could respond to changing demand. Research on extended reality also reminds us that network performance should be assessed through human experience, not only data rates. A useful evaluation would measure interaction delay, interruptions, and user comfort. [2], [5]

【仍在PPT第9页｜中间CASE 02：互联交通；handover指车辆移动时的网络／基站切换】

The second case is connected transport. In the ISAC survey, Liu and colleagues discuss roadside infrastructure that can sense a vehicle's position and motion while supporting communication. Sensing information can help track a narrow radio beam or prepare resources before the vehicle enters another coverage area. This connects sensing directly to a communication problem. However, tracking errors and interference can undermine the benefit. Evaluation should therefore examine both sensing accuracy and connection reliability. [4]

【仍在PPT第9页｜右侧CASE 03：可再生能源协调；供需信息、成本、能耗、兼容性】

The third case is renewable-energy coordination. Rashid and colleagues review how future communication networks could support energy management, distributed generation, and electricity trading. For example, timely information from solar installations, batteries, and consumers could help coordinate local supply and demand. Yet the authors also identify costs, power consumption, and compatibility with older equipment as obstacles. [6]

【仍在PPT第9页｜对应底部蓝色问题：比现有方案带来什么可测量收益？】

These cases suggest an important test: what extra benefit does a proposed 6G design provide compared with an existing solution? A new network should be justified by measurable improvements, not by the generation label alone.

## 5. Challenges and Future Research Directions

讲述人：队友B

【PPT第10页｜队友B接讲｜依次对应SYSTEM、TRUST、IMPACT三个框】

**Thank you.**

Three challenges deserve particular attention.

First, promising components must work together under realistic conditions. A high-capacity link may be sensitive to blockage. A complex AI model may consume more energy than its decisions save. Research should compare complete systems under mobility, congestion, and equipment constraints. Reproducible testbeds and fair baseline comparisons would help show where the benefits actually come from. [2], [3]

Second, intelligence and sensing create security and privacy risks. A network that learns from data can also learn from manipulated data. A network that senses movement may reveal sensitive information about people. Nguyen and colleagues review threats across prospective 6G technologies, including attacks on machine-learning processes. Future research should build protection into system design and test how networks behave when data or models are attacked. [7]

Third, sustainability requires more than transmitting each bit efficiently. Additional infrastructure, computing, and hardware also consume resources. Ahmad and colleagues examine sustainability across network management, security, and hardware. Their analysis supports evaluating resource trade-offs across the system. Future studies should report energy consumption alongside performance and consider the practical cost of deploying and maintaining their designs. [8]

【切到PPT第11页｜三个研究问题：可靠性、抵抗攻击与恢复能力、可持续性】

For these reasons, I would prioritise three research questions: Does the system remain reliable outside ideal conditions? Can it protect users and recover from attacks? And does its benefit justify its energy and deployment costs?

Answering these questions will require collaboration across communications, computing, security, and application design.

## 6. Conclusion

讲述人：队友B

【切到PPT第12页｜队友B继续｜结论；下句是同一人的章节衔接】

**Let me finish with our main message.**

To conclude, 6G research points towards wireless systems that combine communication with sensing, computing, and intelligence. AI-native networking, new radio techniques, ISAC, and broader coverage could support applications ranging from immersive learning to connected transport and energy coordination.

But these opportunities come with difficult trade-offs. Faster transmission alone does not guarantee a dependable application, and a more intelligent network is not automatically secure or sustainable.

The most convincing future for 6G is therefore one demonstrated through useful applications and realistic evidence: better experiences, reliable operation, and responsible use of resources.
